        PairDesk relay

        When your phone can't reach this computer directly (another network, no Tailscale), it connects through the relay. Everything stays encrypted between your phone and this computer: the relay only passes it along and can't see your screen or what you type.

        Relay server

        Tailscale

        Checking for Tailscale…
        1. Install Tailscale on this computer and sign in. It's free for personal use.
        2. Install Tailscale on your phone (Google Play or F-Droid) and sign in with the same account.
        3. Open PairDesk on your phone once while you're on the same Wi-Fi, so it learns this computer's Tailscale address. (Already paired phones are told as soon as Tailscale comes up; you can also just scan a new pairing code.)

        Windows Firewall

        Windows asks which networks an app may be reached on, and leaves "public" ones out. It usually files the Tailscale adapter as public, so phones get through on Wi-Fi but not through Tailscale. This writes a rule for every kind of network instead.

        Your own router instead

        No Tailscale and no relay: a port forward to this computer.

        Your phone and this computer sit on two different private networks, so something has to join them: Tailscale, the PairDesk relay, or your own router. For the last one, give the router a port forward to this computer and tell the phone where to find it. No other company is involved.

        1. Give this computer a fixed address on your home network (your router calls it a DHCP reservation or static lease). It is – right now.
        2. In the router, forward TCP port 47800 to that address.
        3. Your home connection's address changes now and then, so set up a free dynamic DNS name (DuckDNS, No-IP, or your router's own) that follows it.
        4. On the phone, open the computer's ⋯ menu, choose Add an address and type that name. Add :port after it if the router forwards a different outside port.

        This only works if your internet provider gives you a public address. If the address your router shows is different from the one a "what is my IP" page shows, you are behind the provider's own NAT and a port forward can't reach you — use Tailscale or the relay instead.

        Sign-in screen

        The lock screen, the sign-in screen and administrator prompts, from your phone.

        Windows shows the lock screen, the sign-in screen and administrator prompts (UAC) on a protected screen that normal programs can't see or control. Turn this on to use them from your phone too, including at the sign-in screen after a restart.

        How it works

        Windows asks for administrator permission. PairDesk then installs a small helper service ("PairDesk helper") and keeps a copy of your pairing where only Windows itself can read it, so phones you paired can reach the sign-in screen.

        Good to know: like any remote control that can answer administrator prompts, this also means a program already running on your account could answer one. Turn it on if you need it, and keep your phone's app lock on.
